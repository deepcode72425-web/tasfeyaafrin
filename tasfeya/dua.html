Du'as for Knowledge, Rizq & Success

Qur'anic verses and authentic Prophetic supplications for beneficial knowledge, academic excellence, ease in studying, success in exams, and overall success in dunya and akhirah.

If your goal is increased rizq, beneficial knowledge, academic excellence, ease in studying, success in exams, and overall success in dunya and akhirah, there is a strong collection of Qur'anic duas and authentic/accepted Prophetic supplications.

They are separated carefully so you can distinguish Qur'an, Sahih Hadith, and duas whose attribution or strength needs caution.

Important: There is no authentic Islamic formula that guarantees a particular GPA, exam result, job, or amount of money simply by reciting a dua a fixed number of times. Dua should be accompanied by salah, halal effort, studying, discipline, tawakkul, and avoiding haram.

1

The most comprehensive dua for your goals

This is probably the single best dua to make every morning, because it combines knowledge, rizq, and accepted deeds:

اللَّهُمَّ إِنِّي أَسْأَلُكَ عِلْمًا نَافِعًا، وَرِزْقًا طَيِّبًا، وَعَمَلًا مُتَقَبَّلًا

Transliteration: Allahumma inni as'aluka ilman nafi'an, wa rizqan tayyiban, wa 'amalan mutaqabbalan.

Meaning: O Allah, I ask You for beneficial knowledge, good provision, and deeds that are accepted.

When: After Fajr prayer Source: Sunan Ibn Majah 925 — graded Sahih

This is particularly beautiful for a student because it asks Allah for three things simultaneously: beneficial knowledge, halal/good provision, and accepted deeds.

2

Dua for increasing knowledge

This is directly from the Qur'an:

رَبِّ زِدْنِي عِلْمًا

Transliteration: Rabbi zidni 'ilma.

Meaning: “My Lord, increase me in knowledge.”

Qur'an — Surah Taha 20:114

Allah Himself instructed the Prophet ﷺ to say this. For academic life you can say it before studying, before class, before a difficult chapter, before an exam, when you feel you cannot understand something, or after salah. It is extremely short, so make it a constant part of your daily routine.

3

Dua for understanding and making difficult tasks easy

This is the dua of Musa عليه السلام when he was given a difficult responsibility:

رَبِّ اشْرَحْ لِي صَدْرِي ۝ وَيَسِّرْ لِي أَمْرِي ۝ وَاحْلُلْ عُقْدَةً مِنْ لِسَانِي ۝ يَفْقَهُوا قَوْلِي

Transliteration: Rabbishrah li sadri, wa yassir li amri, wahlul 'uqdatan min lisani, yafqahu qawli.

Meaning: “My Lord, expand for me my chest, make my task easy for me, and untie the knot from my tongue so that they may understand my speech.”

Surah Taha 20:25–28

Excellent for students when starting a difficult subject, preparing a presentation, giving a viva, answering an exam, speaking before teachers, defending a thesis or project, or trying to understand complicated material.

4

Dua specifically combining knowledge and rizq

Again, this is worth emphasizing because it directly matches the request:

اللَّهُمَّ إِنِّي أَسْأَلُكَ عِلْمًا نَافِعًا وَرِزْقًا طَيِّبًا وَعَمَلًا مُتَقَبَّلًا

Transliteration: Allahumma inni as'aluka ilman nafi'an, wa rizqan tayyiban, wa 'amalan mutaqabbalan.

Meaning: “O Allah, I ask You for beneficial knowledge, good provision, and accepted deeds.”

Sunan Ibn Majah 925

If you only want to memorize one Prophetic dua, prioritize this one for these particular goals.

5

Dua for halal sufficiency and independence

The Prophet ﷺ used to say:

اللَّهُمَّ إِنِّي أَسْأَلُكَ الْهُدَى وَالتُّقَى وَالْعَفَافَ وَالْغِنَى

Transliteration: Allahumma inni as'alukal-huda, wat-tuqa, wal-'afafa, wal-ghina.

Meaning: “O Allah, I ask You for guidance, piety, chastity, and sufficiency.”

Sahih Muslim; also Jami' at-Tirmidhi 3489

Here al-ghina means sufficiency / self-sufficiency and freedom from need — not merely becoming wealthy. That distinction is important: ask Allah for halal wealth with barakah, rather than wealth that becomes a source of fitnah.

6

Dua asking Allah to provide you with sustenance

The Prophet ﷺ taught a beautiful dua:

اللَّهُمَّ اغْفِرْ لِي، وَارْحَمْنِي، وَعَافِنِي، وَارْزُقْنِي

Transliteration: Allahummaghfir li, warhamni, wa 'afini, warzuqni.

Meaning: “O Allah, forgive me, have mercy upon me, protect me, and provide for me.”

Sahih Muslim; Riyad as-Salihin 1469

Notice how comprehensive it is: Forgiveness → Mercy → Protection → Rizq.

7

Dua of Musa عليه السلام for Allah's provision

This is one of the most famous Qur'anic supplications associated with seeking Allah's goodness and provision.

رَبِّ إِنِّي لِمَا أَنْزَلْتَ إِلَيَّ مِنْ خَيْرٍ فَقِيرٌ

Transliteration: Rabbi inni lima anzalta ilayya min khairin faqir.

Meaning: “My Lord, indeed I am, for whatever good You would send down to me, in need.”

Surah Al-Qasas 28:24

Especially meaningful when looking for employment, seeking financial improvement, starting a business, waiting for an opportunity, struggling financially, or asking Allah for a better future. The verse expresses complete dependence upon Allah.

8

Dua for success in both worlds

رَبَّنَا آتِنَا فِي الدُّنْيَا حَسَنَةً وَفِي الْآخِرَةِ حَسَنَةً وَقِنَا عَذَابَ النَّارِ

Transliteration: Rabbana atina fid-dunya hasanatan, wa fil-akhirati hasanatan, wa qina 'adhaban-nar.

Meaning: “Our Lord, give us good in this world and good in the Hereafter, and protect us from the punishment of the Fire.”

Surah Al-Baqarah 2:201

One of the most comprehensive Qur'anic duas because academic success, career success, halal wealth, family stability and other worldly good can all fall under hasanah fid-dunya.

9

Dua for steadfastness and not losing guidance

Academic and financial success are not the only things worth asking for. Ask Allah not to let success take you away from Him.

رَبَّنَا لَا تُزِغْ قُلُوبَنَا بَعْدَ إِذْ هَدَيْتَنَا وَهَبْ لَنَا مِنْ لَدُنكَ رَحْمَةً ۚ إِنَّكَ أَنتَ الْوَهَّابُ

Transliteration: Rabbana la tuzigh qulubana ba'da idh hadaytana, wa hab lana milladunka rahmah, innaka antal-Wahhab.

Meaning: “Our Lord, do not let our hearts deviate after You have guided us, and grant us mercy from You. Indeed, You are the Bestower.”

Surah Aal 'Imran 3:8

An excellent dua when asking Allah for success with iman and barakah, rather than success that becomes a distraction.

10

Dua for beneficial knowledge — with an authenticity note

You may encounter this longer dua online:

اللَّهُمَّ انْفَعْنِي بِمَا عَلَّمْتَنِي، وَعَلِّمْنِي مَا يَنْفَعُنِي، وَزِدْنِي عِلْمًا

Transliteration: Allahummanfa'ni bima 'allamtani, wa 'allimni ma yanfa'uni, wa zidni 'ilman.

Meaning: “O Allah, benefit me through what You have taught me, teach me what will benefit me, and increase me in knowledge.”

Sunan Ibn Majah 3833 — graded Da'if (weak)

So this particular narration should not be labelled as an authentic hadith. You can still make a personal dua with these words because the meaning is sound, but if your goal is specifically authentic Prophetic duas, prioritize Allahumma inni as'aluka ilman nafi'an... and Rabbi zidni 'ilma.

11

The Prophetic encouragement for seeking knowledge

Although this is not a dua, it is extremely relevant to academic success.

The Prophet ﷺ said: Whoever follows a path seeking knowledge, Allah makes a path to Paradise easy for him.

Sahih Muslim 2699a; also Sunan Abi Dawud 3643 (Sahih by al-Albani)

So your actual studying can itself become an act of worship when your intention is correct.

12

Allah grants understanding as a form of good

The Prophet ﷺ said: “If Allah wants to do good to a person, He makes him comprehend the religion.”

Sahih al-Bukhari 71

Understanding itself is a blessing from Allah. Therefore do not only ask “Ya Allah, let me get a high grade.” Also ask: “Ya Allah, give me understanding, beneficial knowledge, strong memory, wisdom, and the ability to use what I learn for good.”

13

Dua against laziness and obstacles affecting productivity

For academic life, this Prophetic dua is particularly useful:

اللَّهُمَّ إِنِّي أَعُوذُ بِكَ مِنَ الْهَمِّ وَالْحَزَنِ، وَالْعَجْزِ وَالْكَسَلِ، وَالْجُبْنِ وَالْبُخْلِ، وَضَلَعِ الدَّيْنِ وَغَلَبَةِ الرِّجَالِ

Transliteration: Allahumma inni a'udhu bika minal-hammi wal-hazan, wal-'ajzi wal-kasal, wal-jubni wal-bukhl, wa dala'id-dayni wa ghalabatir-rijal.

Meaning: “O Allah, I seek refuge in You from anxiety, grief, incapacity, laziness, cowardice, miserliness, the burden of debt, and being overcome by others.”

Sunan an-Nasa'i 5476

For a student, the phrase wal-'ajzi wal-kasal — incapacity and laziness — is particularly relevant.

A Practical Daily Routine

You do not need to overwhelm yourself with dozens of duas.

After Fajr

  1. Allahumma inni as'aluka ilman nafi'an...
  2. Rabbi zidni 'ilma
  3. Rabbi inni lima anzalta ilayya min khairin faqir
  4. Make your own dua for your specific academic/career goals.

Before studying

  1. Rabbi zidni 'ilma
  2. Rabbishrah li sadri, wa yassir li amri
  3. Then start studying.

Before an exam / presentation

  1. Rabbishrah li sadri, wa yassir li amri...
  2. Rabbi zidni 'ilma
  3. Then rely upon Allah and do your best.

Throughout the day

  1. Allahumma inni as'alukal-huda, wat-tuqa, wal-'afafa, wal-ghina
  2. When struggling with laziness: Allahumma inni a'udhu bika minal-hammi wal-hazan, wal-'ajzi wal-kasal...

One Combined Dua You Can Make Every Day

After making the authentic Qur'anic and Prophetic duas above, you can also make a personal dua in your own language:

“Ya Allah, increase me in beneficial knowledge, understanding, wisdom and memory. Make my studies easy for me. Give me the ability to understand, remember and apply what I learn. Grant me excellent results and success in my education and career if they are good for me. Grant me halal, abundant and blessed rizq. Open doors of opportunity for me that I cannot see. Put barakah in my time, knowledge, work and earnings. Protect me from laziness, distraction, failure, arrogance and anything that takes me away from You. Make my knowledge beneficial to me and to others. Grant me success in this dunya and the akhirah. Ameen.”

There is no requirement to use Arabic for a personal dua; you can ask Allah sincerely in your own language.

One important principle

For these particular goals, focus on this combination:

Dua + Salah + Serious study + Halal effort + Avoiding haram + Tawakkul + Consistency

The Prophet ﷺ taught the tremendous virtue of actually walking the path of knowledge: Allah makes the path to Jannah easy for the person who seeks knowledge. So do not think of dua as replacing your studying. Your studying itself can become part of the means through which Allah grants you success.
